ⓘ Map guide

Every operating natural-gas power plant in the US (lower-48: 1277 plants, 476 GW total) is screened for one question: is there enough developable land within 10 km to build a new data center plus enough new solar to serve a flat 24/7 load equal to the plant's capacity, with the gas plant only covering 5% / 10% / 20% backup?

A first-pass land-availability screen — not a cost, dispatch, or interconnection study.

How to use it

  • Search (top) for a place name or lat, lon to drop a pin and zoom there.
  • Click any plant to draw its 10 km circle and shade the land that could host solar.
  • Use the Street / Satellite switch (top-right) to see what's actually on that land.
  • Pick a Region (ISO/RTO) from the dropdown (top-left) to focus on one grid operator (PJM, MISO, CAISO, ERCOT…), or keep All regions.
  • Drag the Hostable-load filter sliders (top-left) to show only plants that can host a data-center of a given size (MW). This uses each plant's solar-limited hostable load (the partial capacity that fits), not full nameplate; reset clears it.
  • Hover/click a marker for its popup with the numbers below, plus links to the site (Google Maps), the plant's EIA record, the source paper, and full data sources.

Terms in the popup

Key point: a plant qualifies when the solar that fits ≥ the solar needed — not when developable MW > nameplate MW. A flat 24/7 load needs roughly 7× its size in solar panels (panels average only ~16% of nameplate, plus overbuild for storage losses and cloudy stretches), so a 1 GW load can need ~7 GW of solar.

Nameplate (MW)
Plant's max rated output; here it's the size of the 24/7 data-center load.
AC capacity factor
Solar output ÷ its theoretical max, from PVWatts/NSRDB. US range here 0.11–0.22 (sunnier Southwest highest). Higher = less solar needed.
Developable land / usable for solar
Developable land × power density (~91 MW/mi²) = the solar the land could fit. But a fixed 150-acre parcel is reserved for the data center building, so usable for solar = (land − 150 ac) × power density. On small urban sites this deduction can matter; on normal sites the solar dwarfs it.
Solar needed
= 1.3 × (1 − g) / CF × nameplate. The panels required so annual solar energy covers the load (minus the gas share). ~6× nameplate at these capacity factors.
Hostable DC load (solar-limited)
The data-center size the usable solar land can actually power = min(nameplate, usable solar ÷ 1.3(1−g)/CF). Full nameplate for green plants; the partial data center you could still build for orange ones. The hostable-load slider filters on this.
Headroom
Usable solar (after the 150-ac DC parcel) ÷ solar needed. ≥ 1.0 = full nameplate qualifies, exactly; shown per gas cap.
Gas cap (g)
Share of annual data-center energy the gas plant may supply (5% / 10% / 20%). Lower g = cleaner but needs more solar/land.

The test (per plant, per gas cap)

solar needed = 1.3 × (1 − g) / CF × nameplate  ·   land needed = solar ÷ 91 MW/mi² (+ 150-acre data-center parcel). Qualifies when developable land ≥ land needed.

At a glance

744 of 1277 plants fit a full-nameplate data center at the 10% gas cap (conservative, forest-excluded) = 132 GW. But counting the partial data centers that the other sites can still host, total solar-limited hostable load rises to ~240 GW — the opportunity beyond the whole-plant winners.

Read with care

  • Per-plant numbers here are independent and valid. But nearby plants (10 km buffers overlap for ~⅔ of US plants) can share the same land, so national GW totals are an upper bound, not additive potential.
  • The hatch shows the conservative screen (all forest excluded). Including forest roughly doubles the land — a documented sensitivity, not shown here.
  • Land being present ≠ water/fiber/grid capacity being available. This screens land only.
  • Results are a first-pass plausibility screen (land + energy-parity solar sizing), not a feasibility guarantee — no interconnection, dispatch, storage, or cost modeling.
